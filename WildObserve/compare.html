WildObserve™

Camera-trap software compared

WildObserve™, Timelapse, AddaxAI, Wildlife Insights, and AIDE, side by side.

WildObserve™, Timelapse, and AddaxAI run on your own computer. Wildlife Insights is a cloud platform you upload images to, and AIDE is a web app you install on your own server. They differ most in where your images go, whether the AI learns your project's species, and what analysis is built in.

Every entry for another tool comes from its own website or documentation, linked under Sources. A dash (—) means its documentation doesn't say. Last checked November 12, 2026. If something here is wrong or out of date, email info@qsc.earth and we'll correct it.

Which one fits

  • WildObserve™, if you want your images to stay on your computer, a Project Model that learns your project's species as you review, and occupancy and abundance estimates in the same app, on Mac, Windows, or Linux. Having a Project Model in more than 3 projects simultaneously requires a paid plan.
  • Timelapse, if you work on Windows and want free, flexible data entry with fields you design, and you're happy to run detection and classification in other software, such as AddaxAI, and import the results.
  • AddaxAI, if you want a free, open-source app that runs MegaDetector and a choice of regional species models on your own computer. For occupancy, you export your data to R.
  • Wildlife Insights, if you want your images hosted online, with team roles, built-in analytics, and a way to share data publicly. Images are uploaded to the cloud; it's free for most users, and government organizations and companies pay a subscription.
  • AIDE, if you can run a server and want an open-source, web-based labeling tool that trains a model on your annotations as you label. Its built-in models are general-purpose ones that you train, not camera-trap models such as MegaDetector.
